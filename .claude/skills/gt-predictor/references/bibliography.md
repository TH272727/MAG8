The lens's discipline — outside-view base rates, probability scoring, structured analogies, explicit falsifiers — is the tested core of the forecasting literature. Its actor-scoring framework is a stylized cousin of published expected-utility models and is labeled as judgment, not as an estimated parameter.

- **Tetlock, P. E. (2005).** Expert Political Judgment: How Good Is It? How Can We Know? *Princeton University Press.* <https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691178288/expert-political-judgment>
  Across ~28,000 expert predictions over 20 years, political forecasters barely beat chance — and eclectic 'foxes' consistently out-predicted single-framework 'hedgehogs'. Why the lens must argue the opposing case and treat its structural framework as one input among many, never a master key.
- **Tetlock, P. E., & Gardner, D. (2015).** Superforecasting: The Art and Science of Prediction. *Crown.* <https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/227815/superforecasting-by-philip-e-tetlock-and-dan-gardner/>
  Forecasters who decompose questions, start from outside-view base rates, use granular probabilities, and update relentlessly beat expert benchmarks by wide margins. The working practices the lens enforces on every forecast.
- **Mellers, B., et al. (incl. Tetlock, P. E.) (2014).** Psychological Strategies for Winning a Geopolitical Forecasting Tournament. *Psychological Science, 25(5), 1106–1115.* <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797614524255>
  In a two-year government-sponsored tournament, brief probability training, teaming, and tracking top performers each measurably improved geopolitical forecast accuracy. Evidence that forecasting discipline is trainable and measurable — the basis for scoring the lens's own calls.
- **Brier, G. W. (1950).** Verification of Forecasts Expressed in Terms of Probability. *Monthly Weather Review, 78(1), 1–3.* <https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/78/1/1520-0493_1950_078_0001_vofeit_2_0_co_2.xml>
  Introduced the quadratic scoring rule (the Brier score) that rewards probability forecasts for being both calibrated and decisive. The lens keeps a Brier-scored prediction log, so its hit rate is measured rather than asserted.
- **Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. (1979).** Intuitive Prediction: Biases and Corrective Procedures. *TIMS Studies in Management Science, 12, 313–327.* <https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA047747.pdf>
  Intuitive forecasts neglect distributional evidence; the corrective 'outside view' anchors predictions on a reference class of similar past cases before adjusting for specifics. The mandatory base-rate anchor that precedes any case-specific reasoning in the lens.
- **Flyvbjerg, B. (2006).** From Nobel Prize to Project Management: Getting Risks Right. *Project Management Journal, 37(3), 5–15.* <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/875697280603700302>
  Turned the outside view into a working method — reference-class forecasting — that predicts outcomes from the distribution of comparable past cases, bypassing optimism bias. The practical reference-class procedure the lens follows.
- **Turchin, P., & Nefedov, S. A. (2009).** Secular Cycles. *Princeton University Press.* <https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691136967/secular-cycles>
  Quantitative history across Rome, France, England, and Russia shows recurring multi-generational cycles of expansion, elite overproduction, state stress, and crisis. The elite-overproduction heuristic in the lens's framework is drawn from structural-demographic theory — used as one input, not a law of nature.
- **Bueno de Mesquita, B. (2011).** A New Model for Predicting Policy Choices: Preliminary Tests. *Conflict Management and Peace Science, 28(1), 65–87.* <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0738894210388127>
  The expected-utility line of policy-forecasting models (from The War Trap, 1981, onward) scores actors on position, salience, and potential influence (plus resolve); a declassified CIA evaluation found such forecasts accurate ~90% of the time — matching traditional analysis on hit rate while delivering far more specific predictions. The lens's actor-map scoring is a stylized cousin of these models; the mapping — and where it departs (its coordination score has no direct analog) — is documented in the lens's own rubric.
- **Green, K. C. (2005).** Game Theory, Simulated Interaction, and Unaided Judgement for Forecasting Decisions in Conflicts: Further Evidence. *International Journal of Forecasting, 21(3), 463–472.* <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207005000348>
  Across eight real conflicts, game theorists' unaided predictions were right ~31% of the time — no better than novices and near the ~28% chance line — while simulated interaction (role-playing the parties) reached ~62%. Why the lens distrusts unaided game-theoretic intuition and simulates actors' decisions for high-stakes calls.
- **Green, K. C., & Armstrong, J. S. (2007).** Structured Analogies for Forecasting. *International Journal of Forecasting, 23(3), 365–376.* <https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v23y2007i3p365-376.html>
  Listing analogous past situations, rating similarity, and taking the modal outcome raised conflict-forecast accuracy from ~32% (unaided experts) to 46% — and to ~60% for experts who knew several analogies well. The structured-analogies step the lens runs before forecasting any conflict or standoff.
